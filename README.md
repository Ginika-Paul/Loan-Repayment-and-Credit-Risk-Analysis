## Answers and Key Findings

1. It was observed that **79.99% of loans were paid back**, while **20.01% of loans were not paid back**.

2. It was also observed that borrowers' performance varies among loan grades A, B, C, D, E, and F. People with loan grades A to C have repayment rates greater than 80%, while people with loan grades D, E, and F have repayment rates lower than 80%. The lowest grade, F, has a repayment rate of 67.57%. This shows that borrowers with better loan grades tend to have higher repayment rates.

3. I observed how delinquency relates to repayment outcomes and realized that people with no delinquency history or only one delinquency have repayment rates greater than 80%, while people with 2 to 4 delinquencies have repayment rates lower than 80%. People with 4 delinquencies have a repayment rate of 73.82%, which is the lowest among the groups analyzed. This suggests that borrowers with more delinquency history tend to have lower repayment rates.

   I also found out that people with higher debt-to-income ratios have more difficulty repaying loans. The analysis showed that people with a debt-to-income ratio below 10% have a repayment rate of 90.68%, while people with a ratio of 40% and above have a repayment rate of 56.77%. Borrowers with debt-to-income ratios below 20% have repayment rates greater than 80%, while those with ratios of 20% and above have repayment rates lower than 80%.

4. The analysis showed that **Car and Education loans had some of the highest non-repayment rates and among the largest total loan amounts**. Education, Medical, Vacation, Car, and Debt Consolidation loans all have repayment rates below 80%, with Education having the lowest repayment rate of 77.79%. On the other hand, Other, Business, and Home loans have repayment rates greater than 80%, indicating better repayment performance among these loan purposes.

5. Based on credit scores, the analysis showed that people with higher credit scores tend to have less difficulty repaying loans. Borrowers with credit scores of 670 and above have repayment rates greater than 80%, while those with scores below 670 have repayment rates lower than 80%. Borrowers with credit scores of 580 and below have one of the lowest repayment rates, at 67.57%. This suggests that credit scores can be useful when assessing borrowers' repayment risk.

6. Based on employment status, unemployed people and students have lower repayment rates. Unemployed borrowers have a repayment rate of 18.17%, while students have a repayment rate of 40.72%. On the other hand, self-employed, employed, and retired borrowers have higher repayment rates, with retired borrowers having a repayment rate of 99.49%. This shows that repayment performance varies significantly across employment groups. However, lenders should also consider the number of borrowers in each group before drawing conclusions.

7. Based on delinquency, I analyzed how annual income relates to delinquency and observed that there was not much difference among the income groups. The analysis did not provide enough evidence to conclude that annual income alone strongly determines delinquency. However, borrowers with the highest annual income showed a tendency to have no delinquency history.

## Recommendations

My recommendation is for lenders to consider debt-to-income ratios, credit scores, loan grades, age, employment status, and delinquency history when assessing borrowers' risk. These factors can help lenders identify borrowers who may have difficulty repaying loans and make better-informed lending decisions.

## Conclusion

This analysis showed that loan repayment performance varies across borrowers based on loan grade, age, delinquency history, debt-to-income ratio, loan purpose, credit score, and employment status. Among the factors analyzed, debt-to-income ratio showed a clear relationship with repayment performance, as borrowers with higher ratios had lower repayment rates. Credit scores, loan grades, and delinquency history also provided useful insights into borrower risk.

The findings can help lenders better understand repayment patterns and improve their loan assessment process by considering multiple factors rather than relying on a single factor.
